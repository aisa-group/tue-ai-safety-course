I

The Science of Forecasting

Calibration and scoring

What makes a good forecaster

A forecaster is calibrated if, among all events assigned probability $p$, a fraction $p$ actually occur, for every $p$.

Calibration contrasts sharpness, which measures how concentrated forecasts are.

The goal of good forecasting is to maximize sharpness subject to calibration.

Example reliability diagram. Points on the diagonal are calibrated.

Dawid, “The Well-Calibrated Bayesian,” JASA 1982 · Gneiting, Balabdaoui & Raftery, “Probabilistic Forecasts, Calibration and Sharpness,” JRSS-B 2007

Proper Scoring Rules

Brier score measures the mean squared error of a probability forecast:

$\mathrm{BS} = \tfrac{1}{N}\sum_i (f_i - o_i)^2$

Murphy’s decomposition separates it into three parts:

$\mathrm{BS} = \underbrace{\text{Reliability}}_{\text{calibration gap}} - \underbrace{\text{Resolution}}_{\text{discrimination}} + \underbrace{\text{Uncertainty}}_{\text{base-rate entropy}}$

The log score $-\log p(\text{outcome})$ is strictly proper: expected score is optimised only by reporting the true belief.

A single Chicago forecaster, 1972-76. When a trained forecaster says “30% chance of rain,” it rains about 30% of the time.

Murphy, “A New Vector Partition of the Probability Score,” J. Appl. Meteorology 1973 · Gneiting & Raftery, “Strictly Proper Scoring Rules,” JASA 2007 · calibration.city

"Making Beliefs Pay Rent"

A belief is only valuable if it has predictive power.

If no possible observation would change a belief, it is a floating belief that is not falsifiable.

Yudkowsky, “Making Beliefs Pay Rent (in Anticipated Experiences),” 2007.

Sidenote: How good is AI itself at forecasting?

II

The Engine of Progress

Mechanisms that influence AI Progress

Deep Learning Capabilities Follow Scaling Laws

Hestness et al. 2017: power-law error curves across translation, language, vision, and speech.

Kaplan et al. 2020: loss falls as a power law in compute, data, and parameters, over seven orders of magnitude.

Hestness et al., “Deep Learning Scaling is Predictable, Empirically,” 2017 · Kaplan et al., “Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models,” 2020

Moore’s Law

Transistor density, doubling about every two years.

But, AI compute outpaces Moore’s law by roughly an order of magnitude in exponent. Current growth is mostly scaling (more chips, more spend) of chip count (although chip density is a factor).

Our World in Data, “Moore’s Law” · Epoch AI, “Training compute grows by 4-5x per year”

Algorithmic Progress as a Second Exponential

Compute needed to reach a fixed level of performance falls over time, from better architectures, optimisers, and data quality.

~8 mo
halving time of compute-to-fixed-performance (language)
~3x/yr
algorithmic efficiency gain

This is faster than Moore’s law on its own.

Ho et al. 'Algorithmic Progress in Language Models': Physical compute at ~5x per year, times algorithms at ~3x per year, approximates to 15x per year for frontier models. Over 2012 to 2023 compute contributed more than algorithms, but both compound.

Ho et al., “Algorithmic Progress in Language Models,” 2024 · Erdil & Besiroglu, “Algorithmic Progress in Computer Vision,” 2022 · Epoch AI Trends

Test-Time Compute: A Second Scaling Axis

OpenAI’s o1 (Sept 2024) showed accuracy rising with compute spent at inference, not only in training. On AIME, GPT-4o scored 12%, o1 up to 83%.

Epoch: reasoning-training compute scaled ~10x from o1 to o3, then converges to the base ~4x per year trend within about a year.

Accuracy rises with compute at training (left) and at inference (right).

OpenAI, “Learning to Reason with LLMs,” 2024 · Muennighoff et al., “s1: Simple Test-Time Scaling,” 2025 · Epoch, “How far can reasoning models scale?”

Unhobbling and Overhangs

Some capability gains are suprising and not predictable by scaling.

'Unhobbling'

RLHF, chain-of-thought (~10x effective compute on reasoning), scaffolding, tools, and context length. Features that make existing models usable.

Hardware overhang

Capability latent in existing chips, unlocked by a new method. Example: Use of GPUs for DL in 2012.

Software overhang

A large model whose full ability is only elicited later, by better prompting or scaffolds. Example: Claude Code.

Overhangs are why trend extrapolation tends to undershoot around a paradigm shift. The straight line on the compute axis misses the step change when a latent capability is unlocked.

Aschenbrenner, “Situational Awareness,” 2024

AI Progress as an Extension of Economic Growth

World output was nearly flat for most of history, then bent sharply upward with industrial capitalism. AI compute spending is the newest term in that series.

Two centuries of compounding economic growth.

The optimistic case for continued scaling is that AI is the next phase of this curve.

Our World in Data, “Economic Growth” (world GDP, Maddison Project data)

Electricity

Frontier training sites reached about 1 GW in 2026. Five such sites are online: Anthropic-Amazon, xAI Colossus 2, Microsoft Fayetteville, Meta Prometheus, OpenAI Stargate Abilene.

  • US datacenter power: ~31 GW (2025) heading toward ~66 GW (2027).
  • By 2030, datacenters could draw 9 to 17% of US electricity.
  • Grid interconnection and transformer supply are real limits. Hyperscalers are signing nuclear and gas deals directly.

Per-training-run power on a log axis. The frontier crosses the gigawatt reference lines before 2030.

Epoch AI, Frontier Data Centers Hub · Epoch, “Power demands of frontier AI training”

The Chip Chokepoint: TSMC and Taiwan

TSMC holds about 72% of foundry revenue and over 90% of leading-edge production. Almost all 2nm and most 3nm is in Taiwan through 2026 and 2027.

New bottlenecks: advanced packaging (CoWoS) and high-bandwidth memory (HBM). The four big AI designers used ~12% of advanced logic, but ~90% of CoWoS and ~90% of HBM by value. Both were sold out into 2027.

Epoch AI, “AI chip supply chain constraints” · CSIS, “Taiwan’s Semiconductor Dominance”

Where do wafers come from?

Extreme-ultraviolet lithography is what makes sub-7nm chips possible. One company builds the machines: ASML, in Veldhoven, the Netherlands.

  • About 48 EUV systems shipped in 2025, with 60 or more guided for 2026.
  • China has never received an EUV machine (export controls since ~2019). SMIC is stuck at 7nm and 5nm on older DUV lithography.
One company, one country, one town. The entire frontier uses sixty machines a year.

This is currently Europe’s only point of leverage in the AI supply chain.

ASML Q4 2025 results · export-control background: US-Netherlands agreements, 2019 onward.

Frontier Company Capitalization

Frontier-lab revenue is real and growing fast. Whether it grows fast enough is the open question.

2026 hyperscaler capital spending is about $700B, roughly double 2025. OpenAI has committed on the order of $1.4 trillion in future compute against roughly $25B of current revenue.

  • Forecasters underestimated revenue most of all: \$16B predicted for 2025 versus about $30B actual.
  • The Bank for International Settlements warned of bubble risk in June 2026. A survey found 90% of firms report no productivity impact yet.
If the revenue leg cannot keep pace with the capital expenditure, the flywheel stalls, and the compute trend bends well before physical constraints.

Epoch AI, Anthropic vs OpenAI revenue · Epoch, “How well did forecasters predict 2025 AI progress?”. Revenue accounting is contested.

“It is comparatively easy to make computers exhibit adult level performance on intelligence tests or playing checkers, and difficult or impossible to give them the skills of a one-year-old when it comes to perception and mobility.”
Hans Moravec, Mind Children, 1988

Moravec’s “brain-equivalents” extrapolation, 1998.

Moravec, “Mind Children,” Harvard UP 1988

Separating the Digital from the Physical

Robotics buildout is slow

  • Unitree led 2025 with about 5,500 humanoids shipped.
  • Tesla Optimus: “hundreds” built. Figure: about 40 units at a BMW plant.
  • Real deployment is in the hundreds to low thousands, mostly controlled factory pilots.

Biology has irreducible latency

Expert elicitation finds that experiment design and execution resist acceleration by cognition alone: wet-lab cycles, animal models, and trials have physical time constants.

The “AI as normal technology” view: impact is gated by diffusion and organisational change, not by capability.

A software-only intelligence explosion is compute-bound and could be be fast. Physical and biological R&D is latency-bound and could stay slow.

“Limits to biomedical research acceleration through general-purpose AI,” Sci. Reports 2025 · Narayanan & Kapoor, “AI as Normal Technology,” 2025

Amdahl’s Law

$\text{speedup}(N) = \dfrac{1}{s + (1-s)/N} \le \dfrac{1}{s}$

If a serial fraction $s$ of the work cannot be sped up, the whole process cannot go faster than $1/s$, no matter how much the rest is accelerated. Automating 90% of research leave the remaining 10% as the bottleneck.

Whether this limits AI R&D is disputed:

METR (strict Amdahl)

Take the serial bottleneck seriously: 99% AI R&D automation around 2032.

AI 2027 (algorithmic progress may break it)

Better selection of experiments multiplies progress orthogonally to speed: 5x, 25x, 250x, and SC to ASI in about a year.

Epoch GATE (capital breaks it)

With capital accumulation, ~30% task automation already yields more than 20% output growth. “Baumol effects are overrated?”

Amdahl, 1967 · METR, “A simpler AI timelines model,” 2026 · AI 2027 Takeoff Forecast · Epoch GATE

The Flywheel

Compute grows about 4 to 5 times per year. Moore’s law and the spending driven by a loop based on revenue and revenue projections.

Measuring the Trend

Straight lines on log paper

Benchmarks Fall Faster and Faster

Each successive benchmark reaches human performance faster. Human level is the zero line.

MNIST took decades. ImageNet took years. Recent language benchmarks saturate within one or two model generations.

  • SWE-bench Verified: 4% (2023) to about 90% (2026).
  • GPQA Diamond: above 90%, past the PhD-expert baseline of ~65%.

The pattern makes benchmarks a poor long-term ruler: built to be hard, they stop measuring once solved.

Kiela et al., “Dynabench,” NAACL 2021 · Kiela, “Plotting Progress in AI,” 2023 · Our World in Data

METR: The Length of Task a Model Can Do

METR measures capability in a unit that extrapolates: how long a task takes a human professional.

  • 2019-2025: doubling about every 7 months.
  • Since 2023: to about 4 months.
  • Frontier now: 12 to 17 hours at 50% (Opus 4.6, Mythos).

At a 4-month doubling, week-long and month-long tasks fall in 2026 to 2028.

Kwa et al., “Measuring AI Ability to Complete Long Tasks,” METR 2025 · METR, “Time Horizon 1.1,” Jan 2026. Above 16 hours the measurement is unreliable.

The Horizon Depends on the Domain

Time horizon by domain. Software and reasoning lead; computer-use and driving lag.

The headline curve is measured on software and reasoning tasks. Other domains sit far behind.

  • Agentic GUI use (OSWorld, WebArena): horizons ~40 to 100x shorter, roughly two years behind.
  • Self-driving: a ~20-month doubling, much slower.

METR, “How Does Time Horizon Vary Across Domains?” 2025

Forecasting AI Progress

How do people forecast AI progress based on these mechanisms?

Every generation of AI researchers has been extrapolating the compute curve of its day toward a hard takeoff.

1988-99Moravechuman-level compute in the 2020s-2040s pending

2005KurzweilTuring test 2029, singularity 2045 pending
Compute-anchored forecasts (Moravec, Kurzweil) have aged best, and are not yet resolved.

Good, 1965 · Vinge, 1993 · Moravec, 1998 · Kurzweil, 2001 · Yudkowsky’s later reckoning, 2008

Nick Land, “Meltdown” (1994)

“The story goes like this: Earth is captured by a technocapital singularity as renaissance rationalization and oceanic navigation lock into commoditization take-off. [...] Nothing human makes it out of the near-future.”
Nick Land, CCRU, University of Warwick

Note: This is 'accelerationism', the same 1990s compute extrapolation as Vinge and Moravec, with the valence inverted. Here the singularity is something technocapital does to humanity, and in Land's framing, welcomed.

Forecasts are never separable from culture and embedded into subcultures. The same curves may support optimism, doom, and acceleration depending on local culture.

Land, “Meltdown,” 1994 (in Fanged Noumena, 2011)

Conceptions of the Future

Underneath a disagreement about dates is usually a disagreement about whether the outcome is wanted. The same curve is a promise to one camp and a countdown to another.

restrain AI to protect humanity  ←  ·  →  embrace what comes after

Human primacy

Human consciousness is a “precious flicker of light” not to be risked. Larry Page reportedly called Musk a “speciesist” for it. Musk: “I am pro-human. I f---ing like humanity, dude.”

Careful transition

The rationalist and EA position: advanced AI is a real extinction risk, so making it safe should be “a global priority alongside pandemics and nuclear war.”

Accelerationism (e/acc)

Lean into the “thermodynamic bias towards futures with greater and smarter civilizations.” The direct descendant of Nick Land’s technocapital.

Succession

Rich Sutton: “succession to AI is inevitable” and need not be feared. “We should not resist succession, but embrace and prepare for it.” The transhumanist “mind children” lineage from Moravec.

These are not fringe. The people building and forecasting frontier AI hold all four views. A timeline is never only a timeline.

Isaacson, “Elon Musk,” 2023 (the “speciesist” exchange, dated to ~2013 in the book, ~2015 in some reporting) · CAIS, “Statement on AI Risk,” 2023 · Jezos, “e/acc principles,” 2022 · Sutton, “AI Succession,” WAIC 2023

Forecasting European irrelevance by 2031.

AI 2040: Plan A

Published four days ago by the AI 2027 team. Explicitly a recommendation, not a prediction: the same authors’ positive vision of what should happen instead.

  • US-China research transparency, chip tracking, audited compute sites.
  • A deliberate pause of roughly ten years (2035-2040) before a supervised intelligence explosion.
  • The counterfactual no-deal superintelligence date is 2030.

The plan taxonomy

A cooperate · B fight China · C burn the lead · D race to ASI · S shut it down. Plan A is the authors’ “least bad plan we currently know of.”

The safe corridor: capability rises while safety research and compute governance keep pace. Click for the milestone view, no-deal explosion versus Plan A’s controlled takeoff.

Larsen, Dean, Halstead, Lifland, Greenblatt & Kokotajlo, “AI 2040: Plan A,” 9 July 2026

No brakes from here?

Physics allows about 2 × 1029 FLOP by 2030. Whether that happens depends:

1 · Capital

The bubble question. Could fail soonest if revenue lags the ~$700B/yr of capex.

2 · Power

Epoch’s first physical binder. Grid, permitting, and gigawatt-scale sites.

3 · Chips

Packaging and memory were already the 2025 bottleneck. TSMC and ASML throughput.

4 · Geopolitics

Taiwan as a tail risk; export controls fragmenting the supply chain.

5 · Data & latency

The softest constraints before 2030. They bend the shape, not the trend.

6 · The physical world

Robotics and biology lag regardless.

The forecast for AI progress depends on how these subfactors are estimated.

Epoch AI, “The Future of AI”

Key Takeaways

1 · Forecasting is measurable, and hard

Calibration and proper scoring make forecasting a skill. Yet experts, superforecasters, and markets all underpredicted 2023-2025 AI, and disagree sharply on ten-year questions.

2 · The curves are real

A capability-revenue-compute flywheel, plus a second exponential in algorithms, drives compute at 4-5x per year. Scaling laws and METR horizons extrapolate strikingly straight.

3 · The ceiling is physical, the timing is bottlenecked

Power, chips, ASML, data, and capital set the ceiling. Moravec and Amdahl set the order and the bottlenecks.

4 · The scenarios are where it combines

Situational Awareness, AI 2027, the AI Futures Model, and AI 2040 join the threads. Their authors’ public revisions are the best available model of current forecasting (but potentially culturally biased).
